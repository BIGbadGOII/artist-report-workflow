# Automated Artist Report Specification v0.1

## 1. Product definition

An on-demand, fully automated, English-language research report for contemporary art collectors. A user enters an artist's name, confirms the artist's identity, pays, and receives a source-transparent report within 5–15 minutes.

### User job

Help a collector move from an initial encounter with an artwork to a structured understanding of the artist, then preserve that understanding for future encounters.

### Product promise

> Enter an artist's name and receive a structured, source-transparent report on the artist's practice, career, institutional presence, representation and public-market history.

### Non-goals

The report does not:

- authenticate an artwork;
- conduct purchase due diligence;
- appraise or value a specific work;
- recommend whether to buy, sell or hold;
- predict price appreciation;
- rank artists or produce a composite score;
- substitute for legal, tax, conservation or professional advisory services.

## 2. Delivery model

### Input

Required:

- artist name entered by the user;
- user confirmation of the resolved artist entity before payment.

Optional before payment:

- alternate spelling;
- country, medium or gallery used only to resolve identity.

### Output

- responsive web report;
- saved report in the user's library;
- visible generation timestamp and information cutoff;
- share or export capability may follow after the initial MVP.

### Service-level target

- normal report: 5–15 minutes;
- insufficient coverage: disclosed before payment when detectable;
- generation failure: automatic refund or report credit;
- no undisclosed human-review queue.

## 3. Information architecture

The report uses progressive disclosure. The first screen is concise; evidence and tables expand beneath it.

### 3.1 Report metadata — required

Fields:

- report ID;
- artist entity ID;
- generation timestamp;
- information cutoff;
- report version;
- source count by source tier;
- overall coverage state;
- automated-generation disclosure.

### 3.2 Artist identity — required

Fields:

- preferred display name;
- native-language name when documented;
- aliases and spelling variants;
- birth year and place;
- death year when applicable;
- current base when publicly documented;
- nationality or cultural identification only when sourced and relevant;
- education;
- primary media;
- current representation;
- identity-disambiguation note.

Rules:

- do not infer sensitive identity attributes from appearance or name;
- distinguish birthplace, nationality, citizenship, heritage and current base;
- never merge records solely because names match.

### 3.3 At a glance — required

Target length: 120–200 words.

Contents:

- concise description of the practice;
- main media and recurring concerns;
- one or two documented career anchors;
- current representation and institutional context when available;
- public-market coverage state, not a market judgment.

Prohibited language:

- must-buy, undervalued, overvalued, blue-chip unless directly attributed, safe investment, emerging star, guaranteed, promising return.

### 3.4 Biography and career development — conditional

Use only biographical information that helps explain the practice or career structure.

Possible elements:

- education and formative locations;
- documented intellectual, cultural or material influences;
- early career milestones;
- major relocations;
- turning points in representation, exhibitions or media;
- awards, residencies and commissions.

Do not produce a generic life chronology when no causal link can be supported.

### 3.5 Artistic practice — required

Fields:

- central concerns;
- media and materials;
- recurring motifs or symbols;
- working methods;
- formal characteristics;
- documented influences;
- artist statements;
- independent critical interpretations;
- synthesis generated by the system.

Rules:

- artist statements, gallery interpretation and independent criticism must be labeled separately;
- system synthesis must link to supporting claims;
- avoid presenting interpretation as objective fact.

### 3.6 Practice timeline — conditional

Minimum evidence threshold:

- at least two documented phases;
- dated works or exhibitions supporting each phase;
- a defensible description of change.

Each phase contains:

- start and end dates or approximate range;
- phase label written descriptively, not evaluatively;
- media and formal characteristics;
- recurring subjects;
- representative works or exhibitions;
- evidence explaining the transition.

Do not force early/middle/late phases onto a short career or a practice without clear transitions.

### 3.7 Selected works and bodies of work — conditional

Each item contains:

- title or series name;
- date or date range;
- medium;
- dimensions when relevant and sourced;
- image rights/status metadata;
- concise description;
- role within the practice;
- related exhibition, publication or collection records;
- citations.

Selection criteria:

- representative of a documented phase or recurring concern;
- supported by primary or strong institutional sources;
- not selected only because it achieved a high auction price.

### 3.8 Exhibition history — required

Separate:

- selected solo exhibitions;
- selected group exhibitions;
- biennials and recurring exhibitions;
- institutional commissions;
- current and upcoming exhibitions.

Each record contains:

- title;
- venue;
- venue type;
- city and country;
- start and end date;
- solo/group/commission classification;
- relationship to representing gallery when relevant;
- source.

Rules:

- favor meaningful, verifiable selections over exhaustive lists;
- never classify a commercial gallery as a public institution;
- label upcoming exhibitions as announced, not completed.

### 3.9 Institutional presence — conditional

Fields:

- public museum collections;
- institutional solo exhibitions;
- retrospectives and surveys;
- public commissions;
- catalogue raisonnés or major scholarly publications when applicable.

Rules:

- distinguish public museums from private collections, foundations, corporate collections and galleries;
- a collection claim requires a collection database, institution page or other strong source;
- do not convert institutional presence into a score.

### 3.10 Gallery representation — required when applicable

Fields:

- current representing galleries;
- representation start date when documented;
- office locations;
- solo and group exhibitions with the artist;
- previous representation when strongly sourced;
- representation changes and announcement dates;
- source and current-status verification date.

Rules:

- verify gallery facts against the gallery's current artist roster;
- do not reuse gallery boilerplate without verifying the entity and dates;
- label collaboration separately from representation.

### 3.11 Public-market history — required section, conditional data

The section always appears with one state: `available`, `limited`, `conflicting`, or `not_found`.

When data is available, include:

- data provider and cutoff date;
- first recorded public auction;
- total lots offered;
- total lots sold;
- sell-through rate by lot;
- total public sales value when methodologically available;
- median and average sold price, clearly distinguished;
- annual lot and value history;
- medium distribution;
- creation-period distribution;
- auction-house and geography distribution;
- highest public results;
- sample-size warning;
- currency and buyer's-premium methodology.

Calculation rules:

- `sell_through_rate = sold_lots / offered_lots`;
- withdrawn and canceled lots must not silently count as unsold;
- price statistics use sold lots only;
- hammer and price-with-premium must never be mixed without labeling;
- original currency and normalized currency must both be retained;
- conversion rate and conversion date must be stored;
- a median requires at least three sold observations;
- trends based on fewer than five observations must be labeled small-sample;
- top results must not be used as a proxy for the whole market.

Prohibited outputs:

- fair value;
- target price;
- buy/sell/hold;
- undervalued/overvalued;
- forecast return;
- investment-grade language.

### 3.12 Recent activity — required

Default window: the most recent 24 months, adjusted when the artist has sparse activity.

Possible items:

- exhibitions;
- institutional acquisitions;
- representation changes;
- commissions;
- publications;
- selected public-market developments.

Every item requires a date, source and event type.

### 3.13 Sources, conflicts and limitations — required

Display:

- all cited sources;
- source tier;
- access date;
- conflicting claims;
- unresolved identity questions;
- missing sections;
- known public-market coverage limitations;
- automated-generation disclosure.

## 4. Evidence model

Every non-trivial factual or analytical statement is stored as a claim.

Required claim fields:

- claim ID;
- statement;
- claim type;
- status;
- supporting source IDs;
- confidence basis;
- related artist/work/exhibition/gallery entities;
- generated-at timestamp.

Claim types:

- `fact`
- `artist_statement`
- `gallery_interpretation`
- `institutional_interpretation`
- `independent_criticism`
- `market_observation`
- `system_synthesis`

Claim status:

- `verified_single_source`
- `verified_multiple_sources`
- `conflicting`
- `unverified`
- `not_found`

The UI must never convert claim confidence into an artist score.

## 5. Source hierarchy

### Tier 1 — primary and authoritative

- artist or estate website;
- representing gallery artist page and official announcements;
- museum collection and exhibition pages;
- biennial or commissioning institution;
- auction-house lot record;
- official interview, publication or catalogue;
- authoritative public registry.

### Tier 2 — strong specialist sources

- established art publications;
- scholarly journals and university publications;
- reputable artist databases with editorial controls;
- recognized public-market databases under valid access terms.

### Tier 3 — discovery sources

- general media;
- search snippets;
- social posts;
- resale listings;
- user-generated encyclopedias.

Tier 3 may locate evidence but should not independently support high-impact claims when stronger sources should exist.

## 6. Fully automated generation pipeline

1. Resolve artist identity and present confirmation.
2. Estimate coverage before payment.
3. Retrieve sources in parallel by section.
4. Normalize people, institutions, galleries, works, dates and currencies.
5. Extract claims with source spans.
6. Resolve duplicates and surface conflicts.
7. Build practice and career chronology.
8. Calculate market statistics from normalized records.
9. Generate section drafts from verified claims only.
10. Run automated QA.
11. Render report and sources.
12. Store immutable report version and cutoff date.

## 7. Automated QA requirements

### Entity checks

- artist name and life dates agree across sections;
- galleries belong to the correct artist;
- venue names are normalized;
- artworks are not merged solely by title;
- current representation is checked against a recent primary source.

### Arithmetic checks

- sold lots cannot exceed offered lots;
- section totals reconcile with category totals;
- sell-through recalculates correctly;
- high estimate is not below low estimate;
- converted values match stored exchange rates;
- averages and medians use the documented population.

### Chronology checks

- education, exhibitions and works follow possible life dates;
- upcoming events remain in the future relative to the source publication date;
- representation cannot begin after a claimed gallery exhibition without an explanatory distinction;
- phase boundaries do not contradict dated works.

### Attribution checks

- quotations have an exact source;
- interpretive language is attributed or labeled system synthesis;
- promotional copy is not rewritten as independent consensus;
- market claims include sample size and cutoff.

### Template-contamination checks

- no gallery, venue, artist or artwork entity may appear because it existed in a prior report template;
- repeated boilerplate across unrelated artists triggers review by a second automated pass;
- empty titles, placeholder copy and inherited example data cause generation failure.

### Neutrality checks

- block investment and recommendation language;
- block authenticity conclusions;
- block unsupported superlatives;
- distinguish observation from interpretation;
- expose conflicting evidence rather than selecting the most flattering claim.

## 8. Coverage and graceful degradation

### Full coverage

Identity, practice, chronology, works, exhibitions, institutions, representation and market data are sufficiently supported.

### Practice-led coverage

Strong practice and exhibition evidence, limited or no public-market evidence. Deliver a complete practice report and a transparent `not_found` or `limited` market state.

### Market-led coverage

Substantial public-market records but weak reliable biographical or critical sources. Do not invent practice analysis; disclose the imbalance before payment.

### Insufficient coverage

Identity cannot be resolved or too few reliable sources exist. Do not charge, or automatically refund if discovered after payment.

## 9. MVP acceptance criteria

A report passes only when:

- the artist identity is resolved;
- all required sections have a valid state;
- every non-trivial factual claim has at least one source;
- high-impact claims have either multiple sources or a visible single-source label;
- no arithmetic, chronology or template-contamination error remains;
- the report contains no purchase recommendation, appraisal, authentication or artist score;
- generation completes within 15 minutes or the user receives a clear failure/refund state;
- the report is saved with an immutable version and cutoff date.

## 10. Future paid extension: Artwork Context Report

The extension may accept image, title, year, medium, dimensions and edition information. It may contextualize the work within the artist's practice and locate public comparables. It must inherit the same evidence model and must not authenticate, appraise, conduct risk checks or recommend a transaction.
