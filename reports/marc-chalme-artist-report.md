## Artistic practice

### Documented features

- **Media and construction:** The recent documented works are predominantly oil on canvas, ranging from small formats to canvases measuring 160 × 200 cm. Drawings and oil on board also appear in the 2023 Catto presentation. [S4][S9]
- **Subjects:** Exterior streets and buildings, landscape edges, interiors, still lifes, mirrors, curtains, figures, trees, and nocturnal or transitional skies recur across gallery inventories and exhibition texts. [S4][S5][S6]
- **Light and time:** Dusk, night, artificial illumination, shadow, and reflective light are persistent organizing devices. The independent 2022 review emphasizes empty streets and carefully constructed interior/exterior space; the Catto text places many works in a twilight interval between daylight and darkness. [S4][S6]
- **Human presence and absence:** Earlier gallery descriptions include women, children, and domestic moments, while the 2023 Catto exhibition stresses scenes from which people have largely receded. This may indicate a shift in emphasis, but the available evidence is not sufficient to define a formal career phase. [S4][S5]

### Gallery interpretations

M Fine Arts characterizes the work as dreamlike and sometimes eerie, with familiar domestic or street scenes appearing temporally suspended. Catto Gallery interprets the 2023 paintings through “in-between-ness,” a retreat of human presence, and nature poised to enter or reclaim interior space. Catto also proposes affinities with Giorgio de Chirico and René Magritte. These comparisons are gallery interpretations, not independently documented artist statements or established scholarly consensus. [S4][S5]

### Independent criticism

In a 2022 article published by *Galeries & Musées*, Quentin Didier describes Chalmé's practice as fully figurative and centered on the step-by-step construction of space. The article identifies detailed interiors and exteriors, empty streets at dusk or night, and the interaction of shadow and illumination as its main formal components. [S6]

### System synthesis

The evidence supports a practice concerned less with narrative incident than with the psychological charge of a prepared setting. Chalmé repeatedly uses legible, realistically rendered architecture and objects, then removes or minimizes action. Light becomes the principal event: it marks thresholds between inside and outside, day and night, presence and absence. This synthesis is based on the documented work list, the independent 2022 review, and two gallery accounts; it should not be treated as the artist's own stated intention. [S4][S5][S6]
